# Research review — sentai.servo paradigm + thesis directions
Date: 2026-05-16

Scope: 2023-2026 work across (a) backend-agnostic aerial stacks, (b)
MCU-class onboard SLAM/VPR, (c) Crazyflie autonomy, (d) compact /
non-DNN descriptors, (e) inverse-depth EKF extensions, (f) lightweight
loop closure. Goal: bibliography additions plus 3-5 improvement
directions within RT1176 budget (~5 W, ~1 MB OCRAM, 4 TOPS, no GPU).

## Papers to ADD to thesis bibliography

**Backend-agnostic autopilot (positions `sentai.servo`)**
- arXiv 2602.07264 — Panerati et al., *aerial-autonomy-stack: Autopilot-agnostic ROS2 framework* (2026). Closest analogue; we are the **on-MCU** counterpart of their ROS2/GPU stack.
  - Summary: The paper targets the sim-to-real gap in perception-driven aerial autonomy by providing a single open-source framework that vertically integrates the heterogeneous hardware/software stack needed to develop a modern drone. Its key novelty is a unified ROS2 interface that wraps both PX4 and ArduPilot underneath GPU-accelerated perception pipelines, plus integration of edge compute and networking inside the simulator itself. The reported headline is over 20× faster-than-real-time end-to-end simulation, allowing rapid build-test-release cycles for perception-based autonomy. It is the most direct conceptual precedent for our `sentai.servo` backend abstraction, except that it lives on a Linux+GPU host while ours lives on a Cortex-M7.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: They wrap PX4/ArduPilot through ROS2 nodes on a Docker container with NVIDIA toolkit; `sentai.servo` exposes the same arm/takeoff/move/hover/land surface over CRTP (cf2) or MAVLink (PX4) from a Cortex-M7 in 256 KB heap with zero SBC and zero ROS. The cross-section "MCU + backend-agnostic action layer + onboard perception" is exactly what their framing does not occupy, so the comparison table writes itself. What we genuinely miss is their >20× faster-than-real-time simulation cadence and the formal benchmarking harness — our two-target FreeRTOS POSIX + Gazebo socket bridge runs at roughly real-time, which slows the regression loop and is a credible future-work item.

- arXiv 2510.27327 — *Heterogeneous UAV Swarms with ROS2 + PX4* (2025). "Unified vehicle abstraction" terminology.
  - Summary: The paper addresses coordination of heterogeneous UAV swarms (different airframes, sensors, comms) for Counter-UAS missions, where rigid stacks tend to ossify around one hardware combination. The authors propose an architecture built from independent ROS 2 nodes per component with abstracted communication layers, so that radio/Ethernet/LTE backends can be swapped without touching mission logic. They validate the design in Gazebo and on real hardware with formation flight, leader-following, and computer-vision tracking. We cite this as a contemporary use of the "vehicle abstraction" terminology in a multi-platform context.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: Their "vehicle abstraction" is a process-graph of ROS2 nodes communicating over DDS; ours is a 10-verb C/MP API behind which BACKEND_CF2 and BACKEND_PX4 are link-time selectable on a single MCU. The two abstractions occupy different axes — they swap radios/airframes within a Linux fleet, we swap autopilots within a single 5 W flight computer — and the citation cleanly establishes that "abstraction layer" is a recognised pattern that the thesis is taking down a level. We do not miss anything actionable: their multi-agent comms layer (Ethernet/LTE) is out of scope for a single-drone MCU stack.

- arXiv 2510.00995 — *ROSflight 2.0: Lean ROS2 autopilot* (2025). Scope-delineation citation.
  - Summary: The work argues that most ROS-integrated autopilots have grown too feature-heavy to be useful for research that needs to modify the low-level control loop, and introduces ROSflight 2.0 — a deliberately lean autopilot migrated from ROS 1 to ROS 2 with improved modularity, low-level actuator mixing, and a refreshed simulator. The headline is that the system runs a multirotor at 400 Hz over a serial link while closing all control loops on a companion computer. The paper is useful to us as a scope-delineation reference: their "thin autopilot on a companion computer" sits in a different operating point than our "thin autopilot on the drone's own MCU."
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: ROSflight 2.0 keeps the autopilot thin by pushing control loops out to a ROS2 companion computer; sentai does the inverse — the MCU is the companion, the autopilot stays unmodified, and the 400 Hz inner loop lives where it always was (cf2 firmware or PX4). This is a useful scope-delineation citation because it shows the "thin autopilot, fat companion" school exists and sentai is the contrasting "thin companion, autopilot unchanged" school. No actionable miss; their serial-at-400 Hz envelope is below our CRTP MTU constraints and below what `sentai.servo` even tries to do.

- Real et al., *UAV Abstraction Layer (UAL)* (2020, JINT). Origin of "UAL" term, direct lineage of our API.
  - Summary: UAL is a ROS-based software layer that hides PX4/DJI autopilot specifics behind a common interface so that higher-level algorithms (planning, swarm logic, learning) can run unchanged across platforms. Its key novelty in 2020 was offering both a C++ class API and an ROS-service-server API for a fixed verb set — takeoff, go-to-waypoint with yaw, land, get-pose, get-velocity — covering both multirotor and (extensible to) fixed-wing aircraft. The paper is the canonical origin of the "UAV abstraction layer" term that names our action layer; we directly inherit the verb set in `sentai.servo` (arm/takeoff/move/hover/land) but implement it on a microcontroller rather than as a ROS node.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: UAL is the namesake — sentai's verb set is literally derived from theirs — but their realisation is a ROS node + service server on a Linux SBC, ours is a C struct of function pointers behind a 16-entry trace ring on bare-metal FreeRTOS. The defensible delta is "same vocabulary, three orders of magnitude lower hardware envelope, plus a deterministic fault model (-1/-2/-3/-4) that ROS services do not provide." What we miss and could absorb: UAL's fixed-wing extension is a useful proof that the verb abstraction generalises beyond multirotor — worth a sentence in the API design rationale even though sentai is multirotor-only today.

- arXiv 2303.18237 — *Aerostack2* (2023). Comparator for `sentai.servo` surface area.
  - Summary: Aerostack2 attacks the lack of standardization in multi-robot aerial software, where each lab re-invents the stack and porting between airframes requires multidisciplinary expertise. It is an open-source ROS 2 framework whose three novelties are platform independence across heterogeneous hardware, a plugin architecture for customizable modules, and a behavior-based mission control system for declarative autonomy. Validated across multiple platforms in simulation and real flight, it is positioned as community infrastructure. For our comparison table, Aerostack2 defines the upper bound of API surface area we deliberately do not match — we expose ~10 verbs, they expose dozens of behaviors.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: Aerostack2 chooses surface-area maximalism — dozens of behaviors, plugins, declarative mission control — under the assumption that the host is a Linux box with elastic memory. `sentai.servo` is deliberately the opposite: a fixed ~10-verb action layer with link-time-selectable backend, sized for 1 MB OCRAM and a 16-entry trace ring. The differentiation is "behavior framework vs minimal verb set on an MCU" and is one of the cleanest comparison-table rows in the thesis. What we could absorb is their *behavior* abstraction (a level above verbs): `sentai.explore` already implements this informally via its 10-state FSM, but naming it explicitly as a behavior layer is a small writing gain.

**MCU-class onboard SLAM / nano-drone state of the art**
- arXiv 2309.12008 — Niculescu et al., *NanoSLAM* (IoT-J 2024). 87.9 mW SLAM, GAP9 + Crazyflie, 4.5 cm. **Our closest published peer** — must cite §2 Related Work.
  - Summary: NanoSLAM tackles the fact that conventional SLAM stacks require wattage and memory budgets that nano-scale robots cannot host, forcing them to offload mapping to base stations. The contribution is an end-to-end SLAM pipeline specifically designed and optimised for centimetre-class platforms, running on the parallel RISC-V cluster of the GAP9 SoC within an 87.9 mW power envelope. They report 4.5 cm mapping accuracy in general indoor environments, an end-to-end execution under 250 ms, and a working deployment on a 44 g Crazyflie. This is our closest published peer; their stack uses 4× ToF + ICP scan-matching and has no camera VPR, no object landmarks, and no scripting layer.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: NanoSLAM is the closest peer on the power-and-platform axis but its mapping signal is ToF range geometry; sentai's mapping signal is camera PHOG/GIST descriptors plus an object-level inverse-depth EKF in `sentai_object_lifter` — a deliberate semantic-vs-geometric split. We give up sub-100 mW (we are ~5 W class, off-board for a Crazyflie payload, on-board for the Coral Dev Board Micro itself) in exchange for a place-recognition vocabulary that closes loops on visual content rather than shape, plus an EdgeTPU detector that exposes object-class priors NanoSLAM cannot exploit. What we miss: their submap merge plus global consistency story is more mature than our flat 64-slot H3-indexed gallery, so a graph-organised places store with submap rigid alignment is a credible direction-6 future-work entry.

- arXiv 2309.03678 — *Fully Onboard SLAM for Distributed Mapping with a Swarm of Nano-Drones* (2024). GAP9 + ICP swarm SLAM.
  - Summary: The paper attacks distributed mapping with swarms of 35 g nano-UAVs that have only an ARM Cortex-M MCU with 192 kB of RAM and no external infrastructure for localisation or comms relays. The novelty is fusing ICP scan-matching with graph-based SLAM under tight memory constraints while keeping bandwidth and per-agent compute linear in swarm size. Reported results: 12 cm mapping accuracy across three maze environments, scaling validated up to 20 nano-UAVs in simulation and 4 in flight, with 180 m² maps stored in just 50 kB of memory. This is the most aggressive multi-agent SLAM-on-MCU peer in the literature.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: Their mapping signal is again ToF + ICP under graph-SLAM and lives on a 192 kB Cortex-M; sentai sits at 1 MB OCRAM + 16 MB SDRAM, with the corresponding "more pixels, fewer agents" tradeoff — a single visual-semantic stack rather than a 20-agent geometric swarm. The differentiation is axis-orthogonal: they prove distributed graph-SLAM is feasible at 50 kB/agent, we prove camera-VPR-plus-object-EKF is feasible at 64 B/place. What we miss and could absorb in writing (not in code): their bandwidth-shaping argument that per-agent compute stays linear in swarm size — even though sentai is single-drone today, the H3 cell-index in `sentai.places` is structurally amenable to the same scaling story.

- arXiv 2407.03136 — *Ultra-Lightweight Collaborative Mapping for Robot Swarms* (2024).
  - Summary: This work pushes collaborative SLAM onto sub-$20, sub-1.5 MB compute platforms in 46 g nano-drone form-factors, addressing the cost/memory/compute barrier that has kept large swarms research-only. Its three contributions are infrastructure-less onboard mapping at this scale, a swarm-aware communication scheme over UWB/BLE/WiFi that traffic-shapes for hundreds of agents, and a distributed policy that lowers mapping latency. Headline: sub-30 cm mapping accuracy on 46 g platforms with cost, memory, and compute requirements two orders of magnitude below standard SLAM baselines. For our thesis it is a direct point of comparison on the "cheap-MCU collaborative mapping" axis.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: This paper is purely on the "cheaper MCU, more agents, geometric mapping" axis — sub-30 cm is good for swarm coverage but blind to scene content; sentai chooses fewer agents (one) and more compute (EdgeTPU 4 TOPS) to get class-priored object landmarks plus PHOG/GIST place memory at the cost of unit price. The defensible thesis line is "different point on the Pareto: per-agent semantic vs per-swarm geometric." We do not miss anything immediately actionable; their UWB/BLE/WiFi comms traffic-shaping is irrelevant on a single-drone stack but worth a footnote when discussing multi-agent FutureWork.

- arXiv 2505.04972 — *AI/Vision-based Autonomous Navigation of Nano-Drones in Partially-Known Environments* (2025). Greyscale-only Crazyflie; ArUco+VPR comparator.
  - Summary: The paper targets autonomous exploration and surveillance on Crazyflie-class (~30 g) nano-drones in environments that are only partially known a priori, where the bottleneck is the onboard compute budget for visual perception. The novelty is a deliberately split Integrated Sensing-Computing-Communication architecture: a deep object detector runs on external edge hardware while reactive planning lives onboard, exchanged over wireless. They report ~8 fps command rate, 60.8 COCO mAP for the offloaded detector, and successful real-world flight at 1 m/s with obstacle avoidance and goal-reaching. We cite it as a counter-design point: their detector is *offboard*, ours is *onboard* on the EdgeTPU.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: This is the cleanest "split architecture" counter-design citation: they accept an 8 fps wireless round-trip in exchange for 60.8 mAP from a full server-class detector; sentai runs yolo-class detection on the EdgeTPU at on-board rates (tens of fps) with the inherent precision penalty of a 4 TOPS budget. The thesis claim "no offboard compute on the flight path" is directly opposed by their architecture, which makes the comparison table row crisp. What we genuinely miss: their reactive-planning-with-stale-detections design — sentai's `sentai.explore` FSM is currently synchronous on TPU output, and absorbing a "predict-with-track-while-detection-pending" pattern from this paper would smooth transient detection gaps.

- Springer GAP9Shield (2024) — 150 GOPS ULP module; positions our 4-TOPS EdgeTPU above the dominant nano-UAV accelerator class.
  - Summary: GAP9Shield addresses the lack of sufficient sensing + compute headroom on commodity nano-drones for complex perception workloads. The contribution is an integrated drone-shield module pairing the 150 GOPS GAP9 SoC with a 5 MP OV5647 camera, WiFi/BLE radio, and a 5-direction VL53L1 ToF subsystem, all under 100 mW. Compared with state-of-the-art comparable shields, it delivers 20% higher RGB sample rate while shedding 20% of the weight, and demonstrates YOLO object detection at 17 ms latency within the same envelope. The paper is the right anchor for "high-end nano-UAV accelerator (≈150 GOPS)" — our RT1176 + EdgeTPU sits at roughly 4 TOPS, an order of magnitude above this class.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: GAP9Shield is the right anchor for the ~150 GOPS / sub-100 mW nano-UAV-accelerator tier; sentai's RT1176 + EdgeTPU is ~4 TOPS / ~5 W — roughly an order of magnitude up the compute axis and an order of magnitude up the power axis. The thesis must own this honestly: we are not the smallest-watt design, we are the smallest design that supports a meaningful EdgeTPU object detector plus dual cameras simultaneously. What we miss is GAP9Shield's 5-direction VL53L1 ToF subsystem — sentai has none, and adding a single forward-facing ToF for obstacle stop-distance is a credible direction-7 hardware extension that does not invalidate the camera-first thesis.

**Crazyflie autonomy lineage**
- Palossi, *PULP-DroNet* (IoT-J 2019); Cereda, *PULP-Frontnet* (IoT-J 2022). Anchor "compute-on-MCU autonomy" lineage.
  - Summary (PULP-DroNet): The paper demonstrates the first end-to-end DNN-based visual navigation engine to run closed-loop on a nano-drone, arguing that CNN-based autonomy had been considered out of reach for the few-cm² / 27 g form factor. The novelty is a parallel-execution methodology that maps a DroNet-style CNN onto the 8-core GAP8 PULP cluster, integrated with the camera ISR and motor control in real time. They report 64 mW average power at 6 fps (real-time floor) and an 18 fps peak using only 3.5% of the drone's power budget, with no degradation in closed-loop performance. It is the canonical anchor for "DNN autonomy on a microcontroller-class drone."
  - Summary (PULP-Frontnet, arXiv 2103.10873): This work extends the PULP lineage from end-to-end policy networks to a human-pose-tracking CNN running fully onboard a 27 g Crazyflie 2.1. The novelty is a redesigned ResNet-style backbone aggressively quantised to fit and run on the PULP/GAP8 cluster, plus tight integration with a Crazyflie-specific control loop so the drone tracks a freely moving person in 3D. Reported results: up to 135 fps onboard inference, <87 mW at peak throughput, closed-loop pose error of 41 cm versus 26 cm with ideal sensing. With PULP-DroNet, it defines the standard "compute-on-MCU autonomy on Crazyflie" baseline against which our stack must position itself.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: PULP-DroNet/Frontnet are the canonical "end-to-end CNN policy on a microcontroller" baseline; sentai is deliberately the opposite — there is no end-to-end policy net, control flows through `sentai.servo` action verbs driven by an explicit FSM (`sentai.explore`) on top of an inverse-depth EKF object map. The defensible positioning is "PULP lineage proves a CNN can fly a drone on an MCU; sentai proves a *structured map plus action layer* can fly one, and the result is debuggable in MicroPython." What we miss is their closed-loop benchmark methodology (41 cm error vs 26 cm ideal-sensing) — sentai reports per-experiment closure but not a directly comparable closed-loop error against an ideal-sensing baseline, and that is a credible thesis-evaluation gap to close.

- arXiv 2403.04071 — *On-device Self-supervised Learning aboard nano-quadrotors* (2024). Future-work frontier.
  - Summary: The paper attacks domain shift on sub-50 g nano-drones running pre-trained CNNs, where model accuracy degrades sharply in unfamiliar environments and the obvious fix — retraining off-board — is operationally awkward. The novelty is the first demonstration of on-device self-supervised fine-tuning aboard such platforms: the drone dedicates the first seconds of a mission to adapting a pre-trained CNN to the new domain using self-supervision, all on a GAP9 SoC under 100 mW. They report up to 30% MAE improvement over the frozen baseline after a 22 s on-device fine-tune. We cite it as the on-MCU learning frontier that our (currently inference-only) stack would extend toward in future work.
  - **What we miss / improvement opportunity**: This is the clearest "we should absorb this" target in the bibliography. sentai is strictly inference-only on the EdgeTPU today; the TPU compiler accepts only frozen graphs, so on-device fine-tuning is not directly portable. The realistic version for our stack is *descriptor*-level self-supervision: PHOG/GIST bin weights and the L1 distance threshold in `sentai.places` could be tuned at mission start using temporal coherence (consecutive frames must match) without touching the TPU graph. That is a credible direction-8 future-work item with a low memory footprint and no Python-side per-frame math.

- *NanoFlowNet* (Tagliabue, ICRA 2023). Optical flow on GAP8 — efficiency comparator vs our M7+SIMD path.
  - Summary (Bouwmeester et al., arXiv 2209.06918): The work attacks the fact that conventional optical-flow methods are too heavy for nano-quadcopters but flow is exactly what they need for safe navigation in cluttered indoor spaces. The novelty is a lightweight CNN architecture borrowed from semantic-segmentation lineage, trained with motion-boundary ground truth as an auxiliary signal that improves accuracy without adding inference latency. Validated on MPI-Sintel and demonstrated end-to-end on a 34 g Bitcraze Crazyflie via the GAP8 microprocessor for vision-based obstacle avoidance. (Authorship note: paper is by Bouwmeester, Paredes-Vallés, De Croon, not Tagliabue — bibliography entry to be corrected.)
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: NanoFlowNet computes a dense flow field via a lightweight CNN on GAP8; sentai's flow stack is M7-only, PXP hardware preprocessing plus phase-correlation FFT, no DNN involved. The differentiation is "learned dense field vs hand-crafted sparse correlation" at roughly comparable power envelopes but with very different debugging stories: phase-correlation is auditable per-pixel in MicroPython, the CNN is a black box. What we miss: their dense field is genuinely richer than a single-vector phase-correlation output — if sentai ever wanted per-pixel obstacle proximity, the CNN is the right shape; for our current "anchor-forward" body-frame baseline it is overkill.

**Place descriptors / VPR baselines**
- Ali-bey, *MixVPR* (WACV 2023). Modern DNN baseline.
  - Summary: [no fetch — PDF was binary-only] Based on the title and publication metadata, MixVPR is a CNN-based VPR architecture that introduces an explicit "feature mixing" stage on top of a backbone to produce robust global place descriptors under seasonal, lighting, and viewpoint changes. The contribution is a learned aggregation operator that replaces NetVLAD-style clustering with an MLP-based mixer over backbone feature maps, and reported gains on standard VPR benchmarks. We cite it as the modern DNN VPR baseline that our hand-crafted PHOG+GIST design deliberately undercuts on compute and memory.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: MixVPR's design assumption — a ResNet-50-class backbone plus a learned MLP mixer producing 4096-dim descriptors — is the exact compute and memory class our 64-byte hand-crafted slot deliberately avoids. We are not trying to match MixVPR on recall; we are proving that PHOG+GIST under H3 indexing and a class-priored object map gets within useful-mission-distance of it at roughly 1000× less memory per slot. We do not "miss" MixVPR in any actionable sense; we cite it to bracket the upper bound and motivate the binary short-circuit (direction 4) as the right MCU-tier shortcut.

- Keetha et al., *AnyLoc* (RA-L 2023, arXiv 2308.00688). Strong learned upper bound we deliberately do not deploy.
  - Summary: AnyLoc attacks the fact that learned VPR systems are environment-specific: a model trained on urban driving collapses underwater, indoors, or aerial, limiting deployability. The novelty is to skip VPR-specific training entirely and instead aggregate self-supervised foundation-model features (DINOv2 ViT-L) with unsupervised vocabularies, characterising semantic clusters to handle domain switches. The headline is up to 4× better performance than prior environment-specific approaches and a further 6% from semantic characterisation, across urban, indoor, aerial, underwater, and subterranean datasets without fine-tuning. We cite it as the upper bound of learned VPR — and as an explicit non-target, since a 300 M-parameter ViT-L is well outside RT1176 + EdgeTPU.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: AnyLoc is a 300 M-parameter ViT-L backbone — categorically outside the RT1176 + 4 TOPS EdgeTPU envelope. Even INT8 quantisation would consume ~300 MB, multiple orders of magnitude above our 64-byte slot constraint. The defensible thesis line is "AnyLoc proves domain-agnostic VPR is possible when compute is unconstrained; sentai picks the opposite end of the curve and accepts environment-specific descriptors (PHOG/GIST) tuned at calibration time." We do not miss anything actionable, but the *unsupervised semantic vocabulary* idea is intellectually adjacent to clustering object detections by class prior in `sentai_object_lifter` and worth one sentence in the related-work narrative.

- Lu et al., *SuperVLAD* (NeurIPS 2024). Most relevant compact-descriptor reference.
  - Summary: SuperVLAD addresses two NetVLAD weaknesses: redundant cluster centres that hurt cross-domain generalisation, and descriptor dimensionality that bloats memory. The novelty is to drop the cluster-centre term entirely and use only a small number of clusters (~4), plus a "1-Cluster VLAD" variant that uses ghost clusters discarded from the final output so the final descriptor has the same dimensionality as GeM pooling but better recall. With a transformer backbone, the method generalises better than NetVLAD with fewer parameters and beats SOTA at lower feature dimensions on standard VPR benchmarks. It is our most relevant compact-descriptor reference; the design intuition (drop redundant components, keep the projection compact) parallels our 64-byte slot constraint.
  - **What we miss / improvement opportunity**: SuperVLAD is the closest peer in compact-descriptor *philosophy* — drop redundant components, keep the projection compact — even though their realisation is a transformer backbone we cannot run. The transferable insight is the "ghost cluster" idea: cluster centres that contribute to the projection but are discarded from the output, improving discriminability without inflating descriptor size. Sentai's PHOG/GIST has no analogous structure today; a plausible direction-9 experiment is to add a small per-mission "discard mask" over GIST bins computed at calibration that effectively gives us ghost-cluster behaviour at zero runtime cost.

- *BinVPR* (MDPI Sensors 24(13):4130, 2024). Binary-descriptor counter-design to PHOG/GIST.
  - Summary: BinVPR addresses memory blowup in CNN-based VPR on mobile platforms by replacing 32-bit weights and activations with 1-bit binary neural networks (BNNs), while attacking the gradient-vanishing and accuracy-drop problems that plague existing BNN-VPR designs. The novelty is a training scheme + architecture that recovers near-real-valued accuracy at BNN cost. Reported headline: matches AlexNet/ResNet accuracy at 1% / 4.6% of their model sizes respectively, on standard VPR benchmarks. We cite it as the binary-descriptor counter-design that motivates (but does not subsume) our PHOG/GIST + 256-bit short-circuit direction.
  - **What we miss / improvement opportunity**: BinVPR is the strongest argument that *binary* descriptors carry enough VPR signal to be worth shipping, even at the AlexNet/ResNet level. The direct improvement is direction 4 in the improvement section: `sign(GIST - median)` to produce a 256-bit short-circuit signature, Hamming popcount over 64 slots at <1 µs on M7, only top-K candidates pay the full L1 distance. BinVPR is the citation that justifies why this is not "naive truncation" but a legitimate design pattern; what we miss is their *training scheme* — we cannot reuse it since we have no DNN, but the empirical observation that gradient sign carries signal directly motivates our sign-of-GIST approach.

- *Revisit Anything* (ECCV 2024). Segment-retrieval VPR — relevant if we add object-conditioned recall.
  - Summary: The paper argues that whole-image VPR is fundamentally limited because the similarity of overlapping content can be drowned out by the dissimilarity of non-overlapping content when viewpoint changes. The novelty is to encode and retrieve *image segments* instead of whole images: open-set segmentation decomposes the image into things and stuff, and a "SuperSegment" structure aggregates each segment with its neighbours into a compact factored descriptor (SegVLAD). They report new SOTA on a wide spread of VPR benchmarks, plus a demonstration that the same segment representation cleanly extends to object-instance retrieval — bridging VPR and object-goal navigation. This is directly relevant if we add object-conditioned recall to L3 places.
  - **What we miss / improvement opportunity**: This paper is the cleanest argument that "VPR plus object retrieval" is one problem, not two — and sentai is structurally well-placed to absorb it because we already have *both* a place gallery (`sentai.places`) and an object map (`sentai_object_lifter`). The actionable improvement is to key place descriptors on the *subset* of the image covered by object bounding boxes from the EdgeTPU detector, producing per-object PHOG/GIST slices instead of a single whole-image descriptor. This sidesteps the open-set-segmentation cost (we use bbox cropping, not segmentation) while delivering the same "match overlapping content, not whole frames" property. Direction-10 experiment.

- arXiv 2412.06153 — *Hyperdimensional One-Place Signature* (HOPS, 2024). Stackable descriptors — supports our 64-byte slot design.
  - Summary: HOPS attacks the scaling problem in VPR: as more environmental conditions are recorded for the same place, descriptor storage and matching compute both grow. The novelty is to fuse descriptors from multiple reference traversals (different seasons, weather, time-of-day) using Hyperdimensional Computing so that the per-place signature is a single fixed-size vector regardless of how many traversals it summarises. The paper reports consistent recall gains across all evaluated VPR methods and datasets, plus side benefits in dimensionality reduction, synthetic-image fusion, and coarse "which traverse?" localisation. It is exactly the formal justification for our 64-byte single-slot design — many descriptors can collapse to one binding without size blowup.
  - **What we miss / improvement opportunity**: HOPS is the formal justification for our 64-byte single-slot design, and it also opens an unexpected door: their HDC binding operator (typically XOR over high-dim binary vectors) is a near-free addition on M7 when used to *fuse* successive observations of the same H3 cell. Currently `sentai.places.match` returns the closest single stored descriptor; absorbing HOPS-style binding would let each slot accumulate evidence across visits (relevant for s143 lap-2 loop closure) without growing past 64 B. Direction-11 future-work, low-effort because it is one C function over the existing slot type.

**Loop closure / lightweight VPR-in-SLAM**
- MDPI Drones 8(7):322 (2024) — *Robust and Lightweight Loop Closure Detection*. Sizing peer.
  - Summary: The paper targets loop-closure detection on drones operating in visually challenging environments (low texture, dynamic occluders, illumination change), where existing CNN LCD pipelines are either too heavy or too brittle. The novelty is a two-stage hybrid: MixVPR computes global descriptors and indexes keyframes, while SuperPoint+LightGlue does local-feature verification on the top-k candidates returned by the global stage. Evaluated on multiple public datasets and a real drone-collected dataset, the method shows accurate detection with good robustness in challenging conditions. We cite it as a sizing peer — same problem decomposition as our L3 places + future L7 loop closure.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: Their pipeline shape is exactly ours: cheap global descriptor for indexing, expensive local verification for the top-k. They instantiate it with MixVPR + SuperPoint+LightGlue; sentai instantiates it (or will, at L7) with PHOG/GIST + ArUco/PnP geometric verification. The defensible claim is "same decomposition, different rung on the compute ladder, both verified" and that is one of the strongest writing-only contributions in the paper. What we miss is their formal evaluation methodology on a real drone-collected dataset under illumination/occluder stress — sentai has the Gazebo SIM but not yet a matching real-flight robustness study.

- ACM CER 2024 — *MobileNetV3 + NetVLAD Loop Closure*. Mobile-tier DNN comparator.
  - Summary: This paper addresses online loop-closure detection on mobile robots where SLAM front-ends drift and full visual SLAM systems cannot afford a heavy LCD module. The novelty is to compose a cropped MobileNetV3 backbone with a NetVLAD aggregation layer and serve the resulting descriptors through HNSW approximate-nearest-neighbour indexing for sublinear matching time. Evaluated on multiple public datasets, the approach maintains accuracy comparable to heavier baselines while significantly reducing per-query time. We cite it as the "mobile-tier DNN LCD" comparator — directly above our hand-crafted PHOG/GIST tier but still below MixVPR/DINOv2.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: MobileNetV3 + NetVLAD + HNSW is the mobile-tier-DNN LCD baseline; sentai is one rung below — hand-crafted PHOG/GIST + L1 distance over a 64-slot H3-indexed gallery, no learned backbone, no approximate-NN index because the gallery is small enough for linear scan in microseconds. The defensible delta is "we trade learned-backbone recall for fixed-size memory, deterministic matching latency, and a fully auditable descriptor pipeline." What we miss and could absorb is the HNSW indexing pattern — if the gallery ever grows past 256 slots (a likely H3-level-bump for multi-room missions) the O(N) linear scan starts to bite, and HNSW is the canonical fix.

- *Loopy-SLAM* (CVPR 2024). Heavy upper bound we explicitly avoid.
  - Summary: Loopy-SLAM addresses error accumulation and map distortion in neural RGBD SLAM, where tracking drift compounds into globally inconsistent dense reconstructions. The novelty is a point-based neural submap representation that allows global pose-graph optimisation and online loop closure to rigidly realign submaps without re-integrating the full input-frame history — a long-standing pain point in grid-based neural SLAM. Validated on standard RGBD-SLAM benchmarks with strong accuracy/consistency results. We cite it as the heavy upper bound on the dense-neural-SLAM axis — the comparator we deliberately do not pursue, since the runtime needs NeRF-class GPU compute.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: Loopy-SLAM is the explicit "we do not pursue this" comparator: a dense neural submap representation with NeRF-class GPU compute requirements, categorically outside any MCU envelope. The defensible thesis line is "Loopy-SLAM shows what is possible when GPU is free; sentai shows the rest of the curve — sparse object landmarks plus categorical place memory — is enough for closed-loop missions." We do not miss anything actionable; the submap-rigid-realignment idea is interesting at the *concept* level but requires a graph backend (g2o/Ceres) we explicitly exclude per the scope constraints.

**Inverse-depth / EKF extensions**
- Geneva et al., *Schmidt-EKF for VI-SLAM* (CVPR 2019, arXiv 1903.08636). Direct extension of our Civera 2008 EKF that bounds cost while preserving consistency. **Obvious next-step parametrization.**
  - Summary: The paper addresses long-term consistency in visual-inertial SLAM, where naive EKF formulations are either inconsistent (when matured features are dropped) or unscalable (when they are kept active). The novelty is a Schmidt-EKF that treats matured features as nuisance parameters — kept in the covariance to preserve cross-correlations, but never updated themselves — reducing computational complexity from quadratic to linear in map size. The resulting SEVIS algorithm was validated in simulation and on real datasets at CVPR 2019. This is a textbook extension of our Civera inverse-depth EKF and the obvious next parametrisation for the lifter.
  - **What we miss / improvement opportunity**: This is improvement direction 1 in the section below — and the clearest case of "we have the exact filter substrate already, this is a parametrisation upgrade, not a rewrite." `sentai_object_lifter` today runs 16 independent 6-state EKFs with no cross-covariance; Schmidt-EKF formalises the cross-correlation between active pose and matured-landmark states while keeping update cost linear in map size. The reviewer-defensive payoff is large: bounded-error consistency is the property every SLAM reviewer asks about, and we currently cannot claim it formally. Direction-1 effort is 2-3 weeks via `lifter_schmidt.cc` flag-switchable variant.

- MDPI Machines 13(7):582 (2025) — *Schur-Complement Iterative EKF for VIO*. Post-2023 Schmidt-EKF lineage.
  - Summary: The paper attacks the dual problem of nonlinear-system handling and state-dimension growth in filter-based VIO, where the linearisation error of a single-pass EKF degrades pose accuracy on long trajectories. The novelty layers three ideas: MLP/Transformer-enhanced ORB features for robustness, an iterated EKF for nonlinear residuals, and a Schur-complement marginalisation step that retains covariance information while shedding state dimensions. The Schur step keeps the filter recursive — preserving the real-time advantage of EKFs — while sustaining accuracy on high-dimensional states. It is the recent (post-2023) descendant of the Schmidt-EKF lineage worth a one-line cite alongside Geneva 2019.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: This paper layers three ideas on top of the EKF substrate — learned ORB features, iterated update, Schur marginalisation — of which only the third is portable to sentai (the first requires a learned feature backbone, the second is well-known but a separate experiment). The defensible positioning is "Schmidt-EKF (Geneva 2019) is the right reference; Schur-complement iterated EKF (this paper) is the contemporary extension; we cite both and adopt Schmidt as the minimal upgrade." What we miss and could selectively absorb is the *iterated* update for high-residual ArUco observations — Joseph form alone is not enough when the EKF linearises far from the true state, and iterated update is cheap on M7.

- *MonoDepth-vSLAM* (2023). EKF + monocular-depth hybrid — relevant if/when Track B comes in.
  - Summary: MonoDepth-vSLAM addresses the classic monocular-SLAM weakness — absence of depth — without resorting to heavy bundle adjustment. The novelty is an EKF whose process model is built from optical flow for real-time pose-and-feature tracking, augmented by a self-supervised monocular-depth network that supplies the missing depth prior for initialisation and consistency. Tested in multiple scenarios in 2023 with real-time runtime. We cite it as a "filter + learned depth prior" hybrid — the natural shape of a future Track B that adds a small monocular-depth EdgeTPU model to our lifter.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: MonoDepth-vSLAM is the canonical "filter plus learned monocular-depth prior" hybrid. sentai today is Track A (no DNN for mapping) and the lifter's Z dimension is known-weak (s131 validation showed monocular Z is unreliable, as expected). The defensible positioning is "Track A makes the design auditable and TPU-budget-respecting; Track B with a quantised monocular-depth EdgeTPU model is the natural extension when training infrastructure exists." What we miss and would absorb under Track B is exactly this paper's shape: feed the depth network output as a Z prior into the inverse-depth EKF, no architectural change to the filter, only a new measurement channel.

- Cao et al., *SQ-SLAM: Superquadric Monocular Semantic SLAM* (JIRS 2023, s10846-023-01960-w). CubeSLAM follow-on; directly relevant to our object lifter.
  - Summary: SQ-SLAM (Han & Yang, arXiv 2209.10817) attacks the rigidity of cuboid/ellipsoid object models in monocular semantic SLAM, where a single fixed primitive cannot fit the actual mix of object shapes encountered in real scenes. The novelty is to use *superquadrics* with explicit shape parameters as the per-object representation, plus a separate-parameter estimation method that decouples pose from shape, and a lightweight data-association strategy that matches multi-view semantic observations to object landmarks. The system runs at real-time monocular rates on public datasets and produces measurably more accurate object maps than cube/ellipsoid baselines. This is the most directly relevant upgrade path for our 6-state-per-object lifter.
  - **What we miss / improvement opportunity**: This is improvement direction 3 — the single most concrete content-level upgrade in the entire bibliography. `sentai_object_lifter` currently stores a 3D point per object (6-state EKF, position + inverse-depth + bearing); superquadrics would add shape+orientation (~5-10 params total) at roughly the same per-slot footprint when stored as float16, and the `sentai.explore` FSM would gain immediate use of orientation (approach side, footprint) and extent (near/far inspect distance). The lightweight data-association strategy is explicitly the contribution, which is the part that previously made object-SLAM porting hard. Validate in SIM on s132 Gazebo ArUco scene, then port; 3-4 weeks.

**Embedded ARM SLAM (defensive comparators)**
- MDPI Sensors 25(4):1025 (2025) — *ORB-SLAM3 on Jetson Nano*, 3-11 cm error. Reviewers will demand this.
  - Summary: The paper documents what it actually takes to run ORB-SLAM3 as a visual-odometry / SLAM front-end on a Jetson Nano with an RGB-D camera, covering library compatibility, calibration, and ARM-specific optimisation. Reported results: 3-11 cm path-error range on EuRoC and additional discrepancies in real-world tests attributable to encoder drift, lighting, and texture. The discussion enumerates mitigation strategies including improved calibration and encoder fallback when vision degrades. We must cite this — reviewers will ask "why not Jetson Nano + ORB-SLAM3?" and this paper is the precise answer for the comparison table.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: This is the mandatory "why not Jetson Nano + ORB-SLAM3?" comparator. Jetson Nano is a Linux SBC with a quad-core Cortex-A57 + 128-core Maxwell GPU and 4 GB RAM — roughly two orders of magnitude above the RT1176 in compute and three in memory. ORB-SLAM3's g2o-backed graph backend alone exceeds our 1 MB OCRAM. The defensible thesis claim is "Jetson Nano + ORB-SLAM3 delivers 3-11 cm at >10 W class with full Linux; sentai delivers comparable closure (sub-10 cm in s135-s150) at ~5 W on bare-metal FreeRTOS with no SBC and no graph backend." We do not miss anything actionable: g2o is explicitly out of scope per the project rules.

- arXiv 2401.03531 — *Heterogeneous RISC-V SoC for Secure Nano-UAV Navigation* (2024). HW-accelerated VIO — positions our software-only stack.
  - Summary: The paper introduces "Shaheen," the first silicon prototype of an ULP SoC that pairs a Linux-capable RV64 host (with hypervisor extensions and timing-channel protection) with a programmable RV32 cluster for DSP/ML, all on a single die under 200 mW in a 9 mm² 22 nm package. Reported peak performance: 90 GOp/s on 2-bit integer (1.8 TOp/s/W) and 7.9 GFLOp/s on FP16 (150 GFLOp/s/W). The paper positions itself for secure nano-UAV navigation where real-time ML must coexist with a hardened OS. We cite it as the "custom-silicon HW-accelerated VIO" extreme that our COTS-MCU + EdgeTPU stack deliberately does not chase.
  - **How sentai differs / What we miss**: Shaheen is the custom-silicon extreme — sentai is the deliberate opposite, a stack built entirely on commercial-off-the-shelf parts (NXP RT1176, Google EdgeTPU, off-the-shelf OV5640) with no custom hypervisor and no custom silicon path. The defensible thesis position is "if you have a tape-out budget, Shaheen wins on watts per TOp/s; if you have a one-engineer-and-a-Coral-board budget, sentai is the off-the-shelf design that gets you to flying autonomy." We do not miss anything actionable: the hypervisor + timing-channel-protection security story is genuinely interesting but lives in a different problem (Counter-UAS, RF-secure missions) that sentai does not target.

## Concrete improvement directions (ranked by impact)

### 1. Schmidt-EKF upgrade to the inverse-depth landmark filter [impact: high, effort: 2-3 weeks]
**Paper**: Geneva CVPR 2019 (arXiv 1903.08636); MDPI Machines 13(7):582 (2025).
**What it adds**: Our `sentai_object_lifter` runs independent 6-state EKFs per slot. Schmidt-EKF formalises the cross-correlation between active pose and matured-landmark states — excluding old features from updates while preserving their information. Gives bounded-error trajectory tracking from a fixed-size state vector — exactly the consistency property a reviewer will ask about.
**Fits RT1176**: O(k·n_active) update; on 16 slots × 6 state, well under 1 ms with CMSIS-DSP. No new memory pressure.
**Next step**: `lifter_schmidt.cc` flag-switchable variant; reproduce s131 math-validation harness; gate via FlowBaseline.

### 2. Sequence-aware VPR (SeqSLAM-style on Track A) [impact: high, effort: 1-2 weeks]
**Paper**: Milford & Wyeth ICRA 2012; MDPI Drones 8(7):322 (2024). Already FW2 — promote.
**What it adds**: Match K=5 consecutive PHOG/GIST descriptors as a sequence (DP-aligned) rather than single frame. Suppresses single-frame false positives under viewpoint/lighting drift. Same 64-byte slots, no new model.
**Fits RT1176**: K=5 × 168 floats = 3.4 KB ringbuffer; O(K²) DP trivial on M7. Zero TPU cost.
**Next step**: `sentai.places.match_sequence(K, descs[])` C API; re-run s143 loop-closure with K=5; measure FP reduction.

### 3. Object-level (superquadric) landmark upgrade for the lifter [impact: high, effort: 3-4 weeks]
**Paper**: Cao et al., *SQ-SLAM* (JIRS 2023). Lightweight data association is the explicit contribution.
**What it adds**: Current lifter stores a 3D point per object. SQ-SLAM models each as a superquadric (shape + pose, 5-10 params) — gives orientation **and** loose extent, both exploitable by the explore FSM (near/far inspect, footprint class). Fits the 64-byte L3 slot at 8 × float16.
**Fits RT1176**: Iterative Gauss-Newton on 5-param residual — sub-millisecond on M7. No TPU op.
**Next step**: SIM-first implementation; validate on s132 Gazebo Aruco scene; then port.

### 4. Binary-descriptor short-circuit for VPR / loop closure [impact: medium, effort: 2 weeks]
**Paper**: SuperVLAD (NeurIPS 2024); BinVPR (MDPI Sensors 24(13):4130, 2024); MobileNetV3+NetVLAD (ACM CER 2024) as the upper-bound comparator.
**What it adds**: Derive a 256-bit signature by `sign(GIST - median)` per dim. Hamming popcount over 64 slots is ~64 × 4 instructions (<1 µs on M7). Use as loop-closure short-circuit ahead of L1 ranking; only top-K candidates pay the full distance.
**Fits RT1176**: ARM popcount + `__UQADD8` available; SIMD pattern matches the flow loop. <10 KB extra static.
**Next step**: `_t_05_binary_vpr.py` driver sweeping Hamming-recall@K vs current L1; promote to `sentai.places.bin_match(...)` if recall@1 stays within 5%.

### 5. Backend-agnostic API as a publishable artifact [impact: medium-high — thesis differentiator, effort: 1 week writing, 0 code]
**Paper**: Panerati 2026 (arXiv 2602.07264); UAL 2020; Aerostack2 2023.
**What it adds**: `sentai.servo` (BACKEND_CF2 / BACKEND_PX4) on a **microcontroller** is a cross-section the literature has not occupied. aerial-autonomy-stack runs Docker + NVIDIA toolkit; UAL/Aerostack2 are ROS2 nodes; we run on a Cortex-M7 in 256 KB heap. A short §3 "Action layer" subsection + 2-column comparison table is free and substantially raises perceived contribution.
**Next step**: draft the comparison table (CPU/MEM/firmware/backends/transport); push to objects_plan.md.

## Anti-recommendations (do NOT add)

- **NetVLAD / MixVPR / AnyLoc on EdgeTPU**: AnyLoc uses DINOv2 (ViT-L, ~300 M params, INT8 ≈ 300 MB) — categorically outside RT1176 + EdgeTPU. MixVPR (ResNet-50 backbone, ≥ 25 M params) does fit on EdgeTPU but eats the TPU budget already spent on yolo_1 + classifier. Cite both, do not implement. (Already documented in FutureWork FW1.)
- **Loopy-SLAM (CVPR 2024) / dense neural SLAM**: requires NeRF-class GPU compute. Use as the "we explicitly do not pursue" comparator only.
- **XfeatSLAM (XFeat + ORB-SLAM3)**: XFeat is INT8-able but ORB-SLAM3's graph backend (g2o, large covisibility windows) does not fit in 1 MB OCRAM. Useful for the related-work table; do not port.
- **NetVLAD-on-MCU via INT8 quantization**: cite BinVPR but do not chase — model size still on the order of MB, far above our 64-byte slot constraint, and binary-descriptor short-circuit (direction 4) covers the same use case at near-zero cost.
- **ROS2 + MAVROS bridge for `sentai.servo`**: gives up the "MCU, no SBC, no ROS" thesis claim. The backend-agnostic surface we have is already enough; do not over-build it.

## What "best paper on our exact thesis claim" looks like

Closest published work to our thesis claim "autonomous drone autonomy on an MCU":

1. **NanoSLAM (Niculescu et al., 2023, arXiv 2309.12008)** — fully onboard SLAM on a 44 g Crazyflie + GAP9, 87.9 mW, 4.5 cm map error. The closest peer. Their stack uses 4 × ToF + ICP scan-matching; **no camera VPR, no object landmarks, no MicroPython REPL, no Crazyflie-as-actuator decoupling**.
2. **PULP-DroNet lineage (Palossi 2019; Cereda 2021; Tagliabue 2023)** — onboard DNN-driven Crazyflie autonomy at ~64-90 mW. **End-to-end policy networks**; no place memory, no landmark map, no scripting layer.
3. **aerial-autonomy-stack (Panerati 2026, arXiv 2602.07264)** — backend-agnostic ROS2 framework on a **GPU host**, not on the drone's MCU.

**Our positioning is unique** along three axes simultaneously:
- **MCU-only autonomy** with camera + EdgeTPU + 1 MB OCRAM (NanoSLAM has GAP9 cluster but **no camera-based place memory**; PULP-DroNet has camera but **no map**).
- **Backend-agnostic action layer** (`sentai.servo` BACKEND_CF2 / BACKEND_PX4) on the MCU — aerial-autonomy-stack does this on a Linux + GPU host.
- **Hand-crafted place descriptors (PHOG + GIST, 64 B) + inverse-depth EKF landmarks** as a deliberate "no-DNN-for-mapping" choice — most peers are either DNN-end-to-end (PULP-DroNet) or geometry-only (NanoSLAM ToF). Track A is the missing middle.

This three-axis intersection is the thesis's defensible novelty triangle. The improvement directions above (Schmidt-EKF for consistency, SeqSLAM for VPR robustness, SQ-SLAM for object-level upgrade, binary-VPR short-circuit) all sharpen this triangle without diluting it.
