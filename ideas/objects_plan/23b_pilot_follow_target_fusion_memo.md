# Research memo — Target (pilot) state estimation by fusing a monocular detector box, a low-rate external coordinate (GNSS/UWB) and the drone's own EKF pose

Scope: the ESTIMATOR / FUSION layer for a follow-at-safe-distance drone (i.MX RT1176 M7, OV5640 VGA 30 fps, detector 20-30 Hz on Edge TPU, drone pose over CRTP/UART 10-50 Hz, pilot coordinate 1-10 Hz with 0.1-1 s latency). Pure vision tracking is in a separate memo. Date: 2026-11-02. All figures below are as reported by the cited source; where a source does not give a number this is stated.

## 1. Target geolocation from a monocular box

**Ray-ground intersection.** The classical formulation (pixel -> camera ray via intrinsics -> rotate by camera-IMU extrinsics and vehicle attitude -> intersect with the ground plane at the vehicle's known height AGL) is given by Barber, Redding, McLain, Beard & Taylor, *Vision-based target geo-location using a fixed-wing miniature air vehicle*, J. Intell. Robot. Syst. 47(4):361-382, 2006, DOI 10.1007/s10846-006-9088-7 (https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/1502). They identify attitude error, altitude error, camera extrinsic bias and wind-induced heading error as the dominant terms and propose four mitigations: recursive least squares over multiple views, explicit **bias estimation**, flight-path selection, and wind estimation. Redding's companion ACC 2006 paper and his 2005 BYU thesis (https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/etd/558) carry the sensitivity study; the thesis quotes an expected accuracy of ~15.5 m for the fixed-wing MAV (altitude not retrieved here).

A recent, directly comparable monocular/no-gimbal treatment is Alaez, Villadangos, Mygdalis & Pitas, *Real-time object geopositioning from monocular target detection/tracking for aerial cinematography*, IEEE MMSP 2023, DOI 10.1109/MMSP59012.2023.10337638 (https://academica-e.unavarra.es/handle/2454/52125). They assume targets stand on the ground, solve the look-at vector length by flat-ground intersection, and build an error model with four terms: bounding-box detection error, height (AGL) error, pitch and yaw uncertainty. Reported field accuracy: within ~5 m range and ~3 deg angular error at ~100 m target distance (hexacopter). No closer-range figures are reported.

The key geometry for our range (3-10 m, camera height h, depression angle theta to the box foot): r = h / tan(theta), so dr/d(theta) = -(r^2 + h^2)/h. Range error scales with r^2/h for a given pitch error — at r = 5 m, h = 2 m, 1 deg of pitch error gives ~0.25 m; at r = 10 m it gives ~0.9 m. (This is derived here, not quoted from a paper.) Bearing (azimuth) error is only sigma_px/f plus yaw error and is far better conditioned, which is why the design in §8 fuses bearing and range as separate measurements with separate noise.

**Box-height pinhole range** (Z = f*H/h_px with H ~ 1.7 m). Error sources collected in the literature: the height prior (real heights vary; the 2026 preprint by Siktar et al., arXiv:2602.20958, lists "height-dependent monocular bias" as the main limitation of YOLO-pose range), posture / partial visibility, pixel noise amplified by Z/h_px, and lens distortion. A UAV-crowd paper (arXiv:2111.14176, 2021) normalises box heights toward an adult standing height before applying the pinhole model. A gimbal-camera uncertainty analysis for the inverse problem (height from known range) is in *Remote Sensing* 12(21):3602, 2020, DOI 10.3390/rs12213602. No paper found gives a closed-form error budget for Z = fH/h with H = 1.7 m; treat it as a coarse range (sigma ~ 15-20 % from height variability alone, a design assumption, not a published figure).

**Camera-IMU extrinsics and time offset.** Offline: Furgale, Rehder & Siegwart, *Unified temporal and spatial calibration for multi-sensor systems*, IROS 2013 (Kalibr, https://github.com/ethz-asl/kalibr/wiki). Online: Qin & Shen, *Online temporal calibration for monocular visual-inertial systems*, IROS 2018 (arXiv:1808.00692) jointly estimate the time offset inside the VIO optimiser; Li & Mourikis (IJRR 2014) do the same inside an EKF (not retrieved in this pass; cite from memory with care). For our system the camera is rigidly mounted and the IMU is on the Crazyflie, so the relevant "time offset" is the CRTP pose latency; §3 handles it by time-stamping rather than by estimating it online.

## 2. Target motion models for walking people

- Schöller, Aravantinos, Lay & Knoll, *What the constant velocity model can teach us about pedestrian motion prediction*, RA-L / ICRA 2020 (arXiv:1903.07933): a plain CV model beats state-of-the-art neural predictors on standard pedestrian datasets; motion history beyond the last velocity adds little. This is a strong argument for CV (or CV with inflated process noise) as the base model for a 1-3 s horizon.
- Yeom & Cho, *Detection and tracking of moving pedestrians with a small UAV*, Applied Sciences 9(16):3359, 2019, DOI 10.3390/app9163359: pedestrians from a stationary drone at 15 m; an IMM with two **nearly-constant-velocity modes that differ only in process-noise variance** is compared against a single KF and a three-mode IMM. The MDPI snippet attributes ~0.8 m position and ~0.59 m/s velocity RMSE to the two-mode IMM; the exact table was not retrieved — verify before quoting. Moving-platform tracking is left as future work.
- Singer and CT models (MathWorks Sensor Fusion docs, https://www.mathworks.com/help/fusion/ug/tracking-maneuvering-targets.html): Singer = CA with exponentially decaying acceleration (reduces to CA as tau -> inf); CT needs a turn-rate state and is justified for vehicles rather than walkers. No UAV paper found that shows CT/IMM(CV+CT) beating CV for walking people.
- Empirical walking statistics useful for process-noise sizing: Teknomo (arXiv:1610.00029, video-measured, 519 pedestrians): mean speed 1.38 m/s (sd 0.37), mean instantaneous acceleration 0.68 m/s^2 (sd 0.12), max 1.44 m/s^2. Zhejiang's occlusion-aware KF (arXiv:2103.05870, 2021) tracks a walking person with position/velocity/size states and CV dynamics.

Take-away: white-noise-acceleration CV with q such that sigma_a ~ 0.7-1.5 m/s^2 (from the Teknomo numbers) is the literature-backed default; a two-mode IMM (low/high q) is the only upgrade with UAV-pedestrian evidence.

## 3. Multi-rate, multi-latency fusion (OOSM)

- Larsen, Andersen & Ravn, *Incorporation of time delayed measurements in a discrete-time Kalman filter*, IEEE CDC 1998, pp. 3972-3977 (https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/incorporation-of-time-delayed-measurements-in-a-discrete-time-kal/): extrapolate the delayed measurement to the current time using past/present estimates and apply a modified gain; motivated by slow vision. (Poulsen is NOT a co-author of this paper; he is on the related AMC'98 vision+encoder AGV paper.)
- Bar-Shalom, *Update with out-of-sequence measurements in tracking: exact solution*, IEEE TAES 38(3):769-778, 2002: exact one-lag retrodiction; ACC 2005 (https://folk.ntnu.no/skoge/prost/proceedings/acc05/PDFs/Papers/0720_FrB02_2.pdf) shows Larsen's and Challa's methods coincide. Stone Soup's OOSM example (https://stonesoup.readthedocs.io/en/v1.8/auto_examples/oosm/KalmanFilterOOSMExample.html) and MathWorks' OOSM guide summarise the three options: neglect, retrodiction, **buffer + re-propagation**.
- Practical precedent on a flight controller: PX4's Vision Target Estimator (https://docs.px4.io/main/en/advanced_features/vision_target_estimator_advanced.html) keeps a templated OOSM history buffer (`VTEOosm.h`), evaluates the GNSS-vision bias at the vision timestamp, propagates a stale GNSS sample forward with vehicle velocity (bounded by `VTE_M_REC_TOUT`), snapshots state at t_vision, replays in-flight samples and propagates to now, at a 50 Hz core loop. Timeouts are counted from the capture time of the newest fused sample, so a late sample does not extend them.

For a 6-state filter the exact method (ring buffer of (t, x, P) + re-propagation) is cheap enough on the M7 (see §9) and avoids Larsen's approximation.

## 4. Fusing a noisy absolute coordinate with precise relative vision

- PX4 VTE (above) is the cleanest public design: per-axis state [r, v_uav, b] with **b = GNSS-frame minus vision-frame bias**, random-walk bias (`VTE_BIAS_UNC` default 0.001 m^2/s, initial var `VTE_BIA_UNC_IN` 1 m^2), vision observes r, GNSS observes r + b, chi-square NIS gate 3.84 per axis. Initialisation: vision-first sets r and b immediately; GNSS-first averages the raw bias through a tau = 0.3 s LPF until 5 consecutive deltas fall below `VTE_BIA_AVG_THR`. During vision dropout, corrected GNSS keeps tracking because b is held. Experimental moving-target build adds [a_t, v_t] with jerk PSD `VTE_ACC_T_UNC`.
- UWB bias states: Goudar, Zhao & Schoellig, *Range-visual-inertial sensor fusion for MAV localization and navigation*, RA-L 2024 (arXiv:2311.09056): dual fixed-lag smoother estimating systematic UWB range biases online, "reduces trajectory estimation error by 50 % compared to not estimating the biases", decimetre-level accuracy, runs on a constrained MAV (platform/runtime not in the pages retrieved). Mueller, Hamer & D'Andrea, ICRA 2015, pp. 1730-1736, is the UWB+IMU EKF that the Crazyflie firmware estimator descends from.
- Barber et al. 2006 (§1) already recommended an explicit bias state for the camera/attitude chain. Together these justify a 2-D bias state b (pilot-GNSS frame -> drone-EKF local frame), observable only when vision and GNSS overlap, held during either dropout.

## 5. Dropout handling, gating, association

- Gating by Mahalanobis distance / NIS with a chi-square threshold is standard (Bar-Shalom, Willett & Tian, *Tracking and Data Fusion*, 2011; METU EE793 notes https://users.metu.edu.tr/home405/umut/wwwhome/ee793/files/METULecture3.pdf; PX4 VTE uses 3.84 for 1-D). M-of-N (or P-of-Q) miss logic for deletion and score-based confirmation are the two classical track-management rules (same sources; MathWorks `trackerGNN`).
- Coasting precedent: Barisic, Car & Bogdan, RED-UAS 2019 (arXiv:2205.00083) run an 8-state CV KF on box centre/size at 20 FPS and feed the *filtered* box to the servo controller, predicting through missed detections. Yu et al., *Autonomous flights in dynamic environments with onboard vision* (arXiv:2103.05870) make the KF occlusion-aware. Tuan & Ahn (§6) switch to GNSS mode after a 5 s vision loss and switch back only when a re-detected box is at a GNSS-consistent distance.
- Dormant-track revival near the last known position and GNSS-based identity are discussed in arXiv:2603.11586 (2026), which also warns that GNSS cannot disambiguate targets ~2 m apart — i.e. with 1-5 m phone GNSS the coordinate separates the pilot from bystanders only when they are several metres apart; UWB (0.1-0.3 m) can.
- A 2020 VISAPP study (https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2020/93556/pdf/index.html) notes operator identification from vision is almost unstudied.

No paper found gives an explicit rule for the covariance level at which a follower should switch to "search"; §8 proposes one as a design choice.

## 6. Published follow-me systems fusing GNSS and vision

- Do (Tuan) & Ahn, *Visual-GPS combined 'follow-me' tracking for selfie drones*, Advanced Robotics 32(19):1047-1060, 2018, DOI 10.1080/01691864.2018.1501278 (conference version KIPS 2017, https://koreascience.kr/article/CFKO201735553776085.pdf): Pixhawk/APM + GoPro on 2-D gimbal + Android GCS. Two modes: vision (TLD tracker) and GPS; switch to GPS if (a) tracker reports loss for ~5 s or (b) box-derived distance disagrees with the GPS-derived distance (wrong-object detector); switch back when TLD re-detects at a consistent distance. GPS mode commands (lon,lat)_GCS + desired_distance * movement_vector — i.e. the standoff point is placed **along the user's GPS-derived heading**. Reported: >50 % better centring than GPS-only, recovery from vision/Wi-Fi failure in 3-7 s (journal) / 5-10 s (conference). It is a mode switch, not a fused estimator.
- Siktar, Caran, Sekoranja & Svaco, arXiv:2602.20958 (2026, submitted to IEEE): EKF fuses YOLO-pose monocular range with depth-camera range on a Jetson Xavier NX at 15 FPS; a GPS-guided approach phase hands over to visual following at a nominal 5 m; up to 15.3 % lower distance RMSE vs single sources. Hand-over logic and standoff geometry are not in the abstract retrieved.
- Vision-only baselines: Pestana et al., SSRR 2013 (IBVS from box position/size at 15-25 Hz); Naseer, Sturm & Cremers, IROS 2013 "FollowMe" (depth camera + EKF); Fast-Tracker 2.0 (IET CSR 2021, arXiv:2103.06522) with occlusion-aware trajectory planning.
- Standoff geometry: Islam et al., *Person following by autonomous robots: a categorical overview* (arXiv:1803.08202) state that behind vs beside is an application-specific choice and that FOV retention is the planner's main constraint. The only GNSS+vision follow-me system found (Tuan & Ahn) uses the heading-aligned point; none compares it against the line-of-sight point.

## 7. Simpler alternatives and learned residuals

- Kalata, *The tracking index*, IEEE TAES AES-20(2):174-182, 1984: alpha-beta(-gamma) gains are the steady-state Kalman gains, parameterised by Lambda = sigma_a T^2 / sigma_z. Comparative studies (warship ARPA 2016, https://koreascience.or.kr/article/CFKO201631751943373.page; air-target simulation https://ohs.elpub.ru/index.php/record/view/13582; Jeong 2017) find the gap negligible for mild manoeuvres and significant for sharp ones, with alpha-beta-gamma showing constant lag on manoeuvring targets. Alpha-beta cannot carry a bias state or a time-varying R from the phone's reported accuracy, which rules it out as the main filter here but makes it a good shadow/sanity filter.
- Learned gain: Revach, Shlezinger et al., *KalmanNet*, IEEE TSP 2022 (arXiv:2107.10043) and the survey *AI-aided Kalman filters* (arXiv:2410.12289). No publication found reporting KalmanNet or a residual-MLP estimator on a Cortex-M class MCU or on a UAV follow-me task; the only MCU KF artefacts found are classical (libfixkalman / minikalman). Verdict: unsupported for this project; keep as FutureWork.

## 8. Proposed estimator for this system

Frame: drone-EKF local NED-ish frame L (origin = Crazyflie EKF origin). Ground plane z = 0 at the pilot's feet (flat-ground assumption; the drone's z from the EKF/altitude).

State (6 or 8): x = [p_n, p_e, v_n, v_e, b_n, b_e, (H, dot-H optional: pilot height as a slow state)]. Default 6 states, H fixed at 1.7 m with inflated range noise.

Process: CV, continuous white-noise acceleration q_a with sigma_a = 1.0 m/s^2 (from §2 statistics; tune in SIM); b random walk q_b = 0.001 m^2/s (PX4 default as a starting point). Optional two-mode IMM (sigma_a 0.5 / 2.0) only if SIM shows CV lag at turns.

Measurements (all time-stamped at capture, processed through the OOSM ring buffer):
1. Vision bearing (20-30 Hz, latency ~ 1 frame + detector ~ 40-60 ms): with d_L = p_target_feet - p_cam, d_C = R_CB R_BL(t_img) d_L, h(x) = [atan2(d_C.x, d_C.z), atan2(d_C.y, d_C.z)] (azimuth, elevation of the box foot). R = diag(sigma_px/f)^2 + J_att Sigma_att J_att^T with Sigma_att from the Crazyflie EKF (or a fixed 1-2 deg). Jacobian is 2x6, analytic.
2. Vision range (same frame, optional, large noise): r_box = f*H/h_px, h(x) = ||p - p_cam_horizontal||, sigma_r = 0.2 * r_box; alternatively the ground-plane range with sigma_r = (r^2+h^2)/h * sigma_pitch. Use whichever has the smaller sigma at the current geometry; or fuse both with NIS gating.
3. Pilot coordinate (1-10 Hz, 0.1-1 s late): z_g = [p_n, p_e] + [b_n, b_e], R from the phone's reported horizontal accuracy (1-5 m GNSS) or UWB (0.1-0.3 m). The coordinate is converted lat/lon -> local ENU at a fixed reference the first time it is received; everything afterwards is absorbed by b.
4. Drone pose from CRTP (10-50 Hz): NOT a state — it is an input, stored in a time-indexed ring and linearly interpolated to each image timestamp (pose latency is measured once with a flashing-LED or motion-step test and subtracted).

Latency: ring buffer of N = 64 (t, x, P) snapshots at the filter tick (50 Hz -> 1.3 s). A measurement with t_meas < t_now is handled by rewinding to the first snapshot before t_meas, applying it, and replaying the stored later measurements (vision ones are kept in a second ring). This is Bar-Shalom's exact solution via re-propagation; Larsen's extrapolation is the fallback if the buffer overflows.

Bias initialisation: vision-first as in PX4 — as soon as one gated vision fix and one GNSS fix overlap within `t_rec`, set b = z_g - p_hat, P_bb = diag(R_g). GNSS-first (pilot out of view at start): run GNSS-only with b frozen at 0 and P_bb large, flag `frame_unaligned`, and let the controller use the coordinate only for yaw-search.

Association when several people are detected: project the predicted state (and, if vision has been lost for > 1 s, the bias-corrected GNSS point z_g - b) into the image, compute NIS per detection, accept the single detection inside the gate (chi-square 2-DOF, 9.21 = 1 %); if two detections fall inside, do not update (coast) and raise `ambiguous` — the wrong update is worse than no update (Tuan & Ahn's distance-consistency check is the same idea).

Dropout policy (design choice, not from literature): coast on CV while sqrt(P_pp trace) < 0.5 * d_standoff; above that, or after 3 s without any gated vision, switch to SEARCH: hold position, yaw toward the bias-corrected GNSS bearing, keep updating with GNSS only (b held). Covariance also drives the standoff margin: d_cmd = d_standoff + k * sqrt(trace P_pp).

Standoff reference: p_ref = p_hat - d_cmd * u, with u = v_hat/|v_hat| when |v_hat| > 0.4 m/s (behind the walking direction, as in Tuan & Ahn's GPS mode), else u = unit(p_hat - p_drone) (line of sight) — blended by a logistic weight on |v_hat| to avoid flipping. Expose both as MicroPython-selectable (`sentai.target.standoff_mode`).

## 9. Compute estimate on the Cortex-M7 (800 MHz, single-precision FPU)

- Predict (6x6 F sparse; P = FPF^T + Q): ~400 FLOPs.
- 2-D update (H 2x6, S 2x2 inverse, K 6x2, Joseph form P): ~900 FLOPs.
- Worst-case OOSM re-propagation: rewind 50 ticks (1 s), re-apply ~30 vision updates: ~50*400 + 30*900 ~ 47 kFLOP.
- Atan2/trig for bearings: 4 calls per vision update.
- At ~1 FLOP/cycle sustained for small non-SIMD float loops, the worst-case update is ~60-80 µs, typical < 5 µs; ring buffers 64 * (6 + 36) floats ~ 10.8 kB (DTCM). Well under the 1 ms budget; place in C (`libs/sentai/sentai_target.c`), expose scalars to MP (no heavy data through MP).

## 10. Follow-up tasks

1. **T-A Camera-pose chain audit** — measure CRTP pose latency and camera time offset (LED-flash / step test), verify R_CB against the existing flow body-frame convention (`cam_mux`, `body_xform`), record in `camera_modules/`.
2. **T-B Standalone C estimator + host unit tests** — the 6-state filter, OOSM ring, NIS gating; replay synthetic trajectories (Teknomo speed/accel statistics) with injected latency; assert NEES/NIS consistency.
3. **T-C SIM world with a walking actor** — Gazebo Garden `<actor>` on a scripted path (straight, turn, stop-go); frames via `gz_to_uds_bridge` only. **Anti-cheat**: the "phone GNSS" must be produced by a host-side phone emulator that reads the actor pose, adds latency (0.1-1 s), noise (1-5 m), and 1-10 Hz decimation, and delivers it to `sentai_sim` through the same CRTP/radio path the real phone would use. This is an external sensor, not ground truth, but it is derived from gz state, so it needs explicit operator approval and a README entry in `sim/ANTI_CHEAT.md` before the experiment runs.
4. **T-D Scoring script (host, post-mortem only)** — `gt_recorder` actor pose vs the estimator's journal; report position RMSE, velocity RMSE, lag at turns, re-acquisition time after induced dropouts, false-association count with a second actor; compare CV vs two-mode IMM, bearing-only vs bearing+range, exact OOSM vs ignoring latency.
5. **T-E Association stress test** — two actors crossing at 1.5 / 3 / 6 m separation with GNSS sigma 2 m and UWB sigma 0.2 m; pass criterion: zero pilot swaps, coast declared when ambiguous.
6. **T-F Standoff geometry A/B** — heading-aligned vs line-of-sight reference point on the same actor path; metrics: distance-hold error, FOV retention, yaw-rate demand.
7. **T-G Shadow alpha-beta filter** — run Kalata-tuned alpha-beta on the vision-only position as a sanity monitor; log disagreement with the EKF (cheap, diagnostic).
8. **T-H Hardware step** — outdoor walk with a phone GNSS logger and the real board; evaluate with a hand-held RTK rover on the pilot as post-mortem truth.

Open gaps in the literature as found today: no paper gives a covariance-threshold rule for follower search mode; no closed-form error budget for Z = fH/h with H = 1.7 m; no CT-vs-CV evidence for walking people from a moving UAV; no MCU deployment of learned Kalman gains.
