# Research memo — continuous single-target visual tracking of a walking pilot from a sub-250 g drone

Scope: vision only (detector + bridging tracker + re-ID + re-acquisition) on i.MX RT1176 (M7 @ 800 MHz) + USB Edge TPU, monocular OV5640 VGA 30 fps, fixed mount. Fusion with the pilot's known coordinates is a separate memo. Hard filters applied: int8 TFLite only, no attention/dynamic-weight ops on the TPU, ≤ 8 MB weights per resident model, a second TPU model costs a weight reload over ~35 MB/s USB. Where a paper does not report a figure, this memo says so. "Est." marks my own estimates, not published numbers.

## 1. Tracking-by-detection vs detection-free bridging

**Foundations.** MOSSE (Bolme et al., CVPR 2010) trains a single-channel correlation filter in the Fourier domain; the KCF paper reports MOSSE at ~615 FPS vs KCF-HOG at ~172 FPS on a desktop CPU and notes speed scales with patch size (Henriques et al., TPAMI 2015, [arXiv 1404.7584](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.7584)). A third-party timing table lists 0.5 ms/frame for MOSSE vs 19.45 ms for KCF, hardware unspecified ([ROBOMECH J. 2021, Table 3](https://robomechjournal.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40648-021-00197-2/tables/3)). Staple (Bertinetto et al., CVPR 2016, arXiv 1512.01355) adds a colour-histogram score to the DCF; CSR-DCF/CSRT (Lukezic et al., CVPR 2017, arXiv 1611.08461) adds spatial reliability masks; DSST (Danelljan et al., BMVC 2014) adds a separate 1-D scale filter and is "somewhat slower than KCF" ([Drones 8(12):700, 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-446X/8/12/700)). The UAV DCF review (Fu et al., 2020, [arXiv 2010.06255](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.06255)) ran AutoTrack/ARCF-H onboard an Intel NUC CPU at ~40–57 FPS. **No paper I found reports KCF/MOSSE/CSRT on a Cortex-M-class core**; the closest are FPGA MOSSE designs ([MathWorks Vision HDL](https://www.mathworks.com/help/visionhdl/ug/object-tracking.html)). Any M7 figure below is therefore an estimate.

**Cadence.** HopTrack (Purdue, [arXiv 2411.00608](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00608)) argues per-frame detection is impractical on embedded devices (60–80 ms/frame on Jetson AGX Xavier) and samples detection frames content-adaptively between a "max rate that holds 30 fps" bound and an "accuracy within 10 %" bound, reporting MOTA 63.12 % on MOT16 at 28–31 fps. A Jetson TX2 retail pipeline runs YOLOv3 on the GPU and Lucas–Kanade sparse flow on the CPU between detections, deleting tracks after 5 consecutive misses ([arXiv 2007.04942](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.04942)). A hybrid broadcast tracker runs Faster R-CNN every N frames and KCF per frame with a cheap per-frame sanity check ([arXiv 2003.03271](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.03271)). A UAV student project runs YOLO every ~20 frames and re-detects when KCF's APCE confidence drops ([UWARG brief](https://uwarg-docs.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/CV/pages/1983250442/Tracking+Research+Brief), non-peer-reviewed).

**Implication for this board.** Our detector already runs at 8–12 ms (320×240) / 20–23 ms (640×480), i.e. every frame at 30 fps is feasible at 320×240 and every second frame at VGA. The cheap bridge is therefore not needed for throughput; it is needed for *latency hiding during detector misses* and for *scale/position continuity across the 1–2 frames the detector skips*. That argues for a sub-millisecond M7 bridge (MOSSE-class or the existing block-SAD / phase-correlation primitives) rather than KCF-HOG or CSRT.

**Edge TPU reality check.** The Coral compiler requires int8 tensors, compile-time-constant tensor sizes and *compile-time-constant model parameters*, and splits the graph at the first unsupported op, with everything after it on the CPU ([Coral model requirements](https://coral.ai/docs/edgetpu/models-intro/)). Every Siamese tracker's cross-correlation uses the *template feature map as the convolution kernel* (dynamic weights), and every transformer tracker multiplies two activation tensors in attention; neither is a constant-parameter op, so none of the trackers above compiles end-to-end. **No int8 Edge TPU port of any SOT tracker was found** (search results only surface SORT-style detection trackers for Coral: [example-object-tracker](https://www.github.com/ivankozlovcodes/example-object-tracker), [edgetpu_roscpp](https://github.com/tongtybj/edgetpu_roscpp)). A workable split is: backbone (MobileNetV3 / FBNet class, ~0.3 GFLOPs, est. 4–8 ms on TPU) → depthwise xcorr on M7 (est. 96 ch × 8×8 template over 16×16 search ≈ 0.5 M MACs, <0.5 ms with SIMD) → head convs on M7 or a second tiny TPU graph. The hidden cost is the **second resident TPU model**: with the detector already loaded, switching models reloads weights over USB (a 1 MB int8 backbone ≈ 30 ms at 35 MB/s) and project history shows a model switch after a TPU fault latches the USB desync (memory: W24-T9). This makes a Siamese bridge on the TPU an engineering risk rather than a drop-in, and favours one TPU model (the detector) plus M7 bridging.

## 3. Single-target lock inside the existing MOT (SentAI-SORT)

**Association baselines.** ByteTrack (Zhang et al., ECCV 2022, arXiv 2110.06864) recovers low-score boxes in a second association pass; OC-SORT (Cao et al., CVPR 2023, arXiv 2203.14360) re-updates the Kalman state along a virtual trajectory when a target reappears after occlusion, correcting accumulated error; Deep OC-SORT ([arXiv 2302.11813](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.11813)) adds camera-motion compensation, *dynamic appearance* (embedding update weighted by detection confidence) and adaptive IoU/appearance weighting, reporting 64.9 HOTA on MOT17; StrongSORT ([arXiv 2202.13514](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.13514)) modernises DeepSORT (Wojke et al., ICIP 2017, arXiv 1703.07402) with EMA appearance banks. A CMU project note observed that naive re-ID *reduced* OC-SORT performance on MOT17/20, which motivated Deep OC-SORT's adaptive weighting ([CMU MSCV 2022](https://mscvprojects.ri.cmu.edu/2022team14/)). Lesson: appearance should gate association only when the detector is confident and the crop is unoccluded.

**Tiny re-ID embeddings.** OSNet (Zhou et al., ICCV 2019, arXiv 1905.00953) model zoo: osnet_x1_0 2.2 M params / 0.98 GFLOPs / Market1501 R1 94.2; osnet_x0_5 0.6 M / 0.27 G / 92.5; **osnet_x0_25 0.2 M / 0.08 GFLOPs / R1 91.2, mAP 75.0** at 256×128 input ([torchreid MODEL_ZOO](https://kaiyangzhou.github.io/deep-person-reid/MODEL_ZOO.html)); the 512-d output is fixed regardless of width ([arXiv 1910.06827](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.06827)). Pure convolutional → TPU-compilable in principle, but again a second model. The zero-extra-cost alternative is a JDE/FairMOT-style embedding head on the detector (Wang et al., ECCV 2020, arXiv 1909.12605; Zhang et al., IJCV 2021, arXiv 2004.01888) — one TPU pass yields boxes and embeddings; it requires retraining the one-class YOLOv5n with an extra head. Classical cues remain competitive for *one* target among few people: Staple-style HSV histograms (already in SentAI-SORT) extended to upper/lower-body part histograms; no microcontroller-class re-ID paper was found in this pass.

**Target selection and lock.** D-PTUAC's authors (Alansari et al., ECCVW 2024, [paper](https://mlanthology.org/eccvw/2024/alansari2024eccvw-autonomous)) select the pilot with RetinaFace + GhostFaceNets and then hand over to a visual tracker + PID; FPS and onboard/offboard are not reported. A ground-robot analogue uses face + torso re-ID and a motion predictor ([arXiv 2309.12479](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.12479)). For this project the pilot is defined externally (REPL / known coordinates), so selection = the track whose predicted ground position is nearest the pilot at lock time; the vision memo's job is to keep that track ID.

**Occlusion and re-acquisition.** Short occlusions: coast the 8-state KF (SentAI-SORT already) with `LOST` grace; OC-SORT's observation-centric re-update on reappearance reduces post-occlusion drift. Long loss: DaSiamRPN (Zhu et al., ECCV 2018, [arXiv 1808.06048](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.06048)) grows the search region by a constant step per failed frame ("local-to-global"); GlobalTrack ([arXiv 1912.08531](https://ar5iv.org/html/1912.08531)) and Siam R-CNN (Voigtlaender et al., CVPR 2020) re-detect over the full frame — here that is simply the full-frame detector we already run. The long-term UAV literature triggers re-detection on a confidence indicator (improved Staple, [Remote Sens. 16(10):1768, 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/16/10/1768); uncertainty-triggered re-detection with +39 % on UAV20L, [Cogn. Comput. 2023](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/22764613); anti-UAV Siamese + hierarchical discriminator, [Sensors 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9143212/)). *Active* re-acquisition by moving the drone: a Search-and-Tracking framework first tracks the predicted location (target cannot have left the area), then runs structured spiral/lawnmower search seeded by last known position and velocity ([AAAI, cdn.aaai.org/ojs/13582](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/13582/13582-40-17100-1-2-20201228.pdf)); a US patent describes delaying corrective manoeuvres after loss, then escalating ([US 12292737](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12292737)). No paper found specifies yaw-sweep parameters for a fixed-camera drone.

## 4. Scale and distance from a monocular box

The two classical cues are box height (d = f·H/h, needs assumed person height) and box-bottom-to-horizon with known camera height and pitch (ground-plane). Monocular 3D human localisation is "fundamentally ill-posed" because of height variation; MonoLoco (Bertoni et al., ICCV 2019, [arXiv 1906.06059](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06059)) and MonoLoco++ ([arXiv 2009.00984](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.00984)) regress distance from 2D keypoints with Laplace uncertainty; MonoLoco++ puts the inherent error at ~1 m for a pedestrian at 20 m. Small box errors cause large distance errors ([arXiv 2101.10399](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.10399)). Keypoint-based methods offset the box bottom because ankles sit above the ground ([arXiv 2104.05813](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.05813)). A 2026 SAR paper fuses YOLO-pose keypoint distance with a depth camera in an EKF at 15 fps on Xavier NX and flags "height-dependent monocular bias" as the main limitation ([arXiv 2602.20958](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20958v1)). Low-cost follow-me systems use box area (Tello, [arXiv 2607.02298](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02298)) or box width (AR.Drone, [Zenodo 15879160](https://zenodo.org/records/15879160)) as the distance proxy. **No paper quantifies crouching or partial occlusion on box-height distance**; geometrically, a crouch to ~60 % height inflates a height-based distance by ~1.6× (my arithmetic), while shoulder width changes little — so width should be the fallback when aspect ratio departs from the walking prior, and the ground-plane cue (box bottom + altitude + pitch from the IMU) is the posture-independent cue when the feet are visible. Fusion belongs to the second memo.

## 5. Published follow-me systems on small platforms

- **PULP-Frontnet** (Palossi et al., IEEE IoT-J 2021, [arXiv 2103.10873](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.10873)): CNN regressing human relative pose (x, y, z, yaw) on GAP8 AI-deck, Crazyflie 2.1, QVGA grey; up to 135 fps at <87 mW; closed-loop following with median position error 41 cm (26 cm with ideal sensing). Failure modes reported: domain shift, single-person assumption (no multi-person disambiguation).
- **Sim-to-real vision+depth fusion** (Crupi et al., 2023, [arXiv 2308.01833](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.01833)) and **state-fusion Frontnet** (Crupi, Cereda, Palossi, 2024, [arXiv 2404.02567](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.02567v1)): 8×8 ToF depth at 15 Hz and drone state fed to the CNN on GAP8 for robustness. A GAP9 result (6.8 fps, 33 mW, 147 ms per 320×240 frame, 9.5× faster than GAP8) appears on an author listing but belongs to a different task ([catalyzex](https://catalyzex.com/author/Luca%20Crupi)); unverified.
- **PULP-Dronet** (Palossi et al., IoT-J 2019) and **NanoFlowNet** ([arXiv 2209.06918](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.06918)) are navigation / dense-flow works on the same class, not person following; the SSD-on-GAP8 exploration paper is object search, not following ([arXiv 2301.12175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.12175)).
- **D-PTUAC system** (Alansari et al., ECCVW 2024): face detection + face re-ID + SOT + PID on a (unspecified) drone; evaluated nine trackers on PTUA / D-PTUAC; concludes current trackers are "applicable and robust enough" for uniform-crowd following; rates not reported.
- All Jetson-class UAV trackers (TCTrack, AVTrack, SiamAPN) are rejected by the hardware filter but their 27–40 fps on AGX Xavier is the reference "what a GPU buys you".

Architecturally, none of the nano-drone works runs a detector + MOT; they regress one person's pose directly and therefore have no multi-person lock or re-acquisition story. This project's detector + SentAI-SORT stack is already ahead on that axis; the gap is the single-target policy layer.

## 6. Evaluation

- **UAV123** (Mueller et al., ECCV 2016; [KAUST page](https://cemse.kaust.edu.sa/ivul/uav123)): 123 sequences, >110 K frames; **UAV20L** = 20 long sequences, mean 2934 frames. The sequence list contains ~23 `personN` and 3 `groupN` sequences by name (from the dataset's own file naming; the count was not confirmed by a publication in this pass). Metrics: OPE success AUC, precision at 20 px, normalised precision.
- **DTB70** (Li & Yeung, AAAI 2017): 70 videos, 15,777 frames, "mainly people and vehicles", emphasises severe camera motion ([survey 2205.04281](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.04281)). NanoTrack's only UAV figure is on DTB70 (0.532 / 0.727).
- **UAVDT** (Du et al., ECCV 2018, [arXiv 1804.00518](https://huggingface.co/papers/1804.00518.md)): 50 SOT sequences, vehicle-centric — low value for person following.
- **VisDrone-SOT** (Zhu et al., TPAMI 2021): 132 sequences / 109,909 frames incl. pedestrians; **UAVTrack112 / 112_L** (Fu et al., 2021): 112 sequences, >60 K frames, long-term variant.
- **LaSOT** (Fan et al., CVPR 2019): 1,550 videos, 70 classes × 20, includes a `person` class; long sequences with out-of-view, good for re-acquisition.
- **D-PTUAC** (Alansari et al., Sci. Data 2024, [PMC10762134](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10762134/)): 138 drone sequences, >121 K frames, 18 attributes, people in identical uniforms, 44 trackers benchmarked and all degrade vs other datasets — the closest public proxy for "pilot among look-alikes". PTUA is its RGB-D ground-robot predecessor (45 seq.).
- **Long-term metrics**: tracking precision / recall / F-score (VOT-LT protocol) and the "3 R's" re-detection, recovery, reliability ([WACV 2020](https://cdn.iiit.ac.in/cdn/cvit.iiit.ac.in/images/ConferencePapers/2020/Exploring-3-RWACV2020.pdf)); for a follower the operational metric is *lock-hold fraction* and *time-to-reacquire*, not AUC.

**Small on-board evaluation.** (i) Run the tracker policy in the x86 `sentai_sim` build on UAV123 person / DTB70 / D-PTUAC subsets with identical source (SIL plan), scoring AUC, ID switches, lock-hold and time-to-reacquire; (ii) on hardware, replay the same sequences through the existing virtual-camera path (memory: "virtual camera off board build") or the display rig used in EXP-s269 to measure true per-frame cost and TPU behaviour; (iii) record our own VGA YUV sequences to SD at reduced rate (full-rate VGA is ~14 MB/s, beyond the JPEG path's 68 ms/frame) with pilot-identity ground truth labelled offline.

## (b) Recommended architecture

Keep exactly one TPU model — the one-class YOLOv5n at 320×240 every frame (or VGA every second frame) — and build the single-target layer on the M7 around SentAI-SORT. Lock policy: a `PILOT` slot bound to one track ID, selected at lock time by the external pilot position; per frame, the pilot box is the matched CONFIRMED track; when the detector misses, a MOSSE/NCC patch tracker (seeded from the last box, PSR-gated) supplies the box for up to ~0.3 s while the KF coasts with IMU camera-motion compensation; on reappearance apply OC-SORT's observation-centric re-update. Identity is enforced with a Deep-OC-SORT-style confidence-weighted appearance bank built from upper/lower-body HSV histograms (already-cheap on M7), and ID switches are vetoed when the candidate violates the ground-plane distance prior (box height *and* width, so a crouch does not look like a retreat). Loss longer than the grace window moves the FSM to `REACQUIRE`: full-frame detections are gated against the KF-predicted position with a search radius that grows per frame (DaSiamRPN local-to-global), then against appearance, and only if the frame has been empty for N frames does the controller yaw toward the last known bearing (staged escalation as in the SAT framework). Optional, later: a FairMOT-style embedding head on the detector to replace histograms at zero TPU cost; a Siamese bridge is not recommended until the two-model TPU switching cost and wedge risk are characterised.

## (c) Follow-up tasks

1. Measure MOSSE (CMSIS-DSP rFFT, 64×64 grey) and NCC/block-SAD patch trackers on M7 in `.ramfunc`; record ms/frame and PSR distribution on recorded pilot sequences (no published Cortex-M figures exist).
2. Add `PILOT` lock FSM + OC-SORT observation-centric re-update + DaSiamRPN-style growing gate to SentAI-SORT; log ID switches and lock-hold fraction in SIM on UAV123 `person*`, DTB70 and D-PTUAC subsets.
3. Quantify the detector's miss profile vs person height in px at 320×240 and VGA (EDAPA frames + own recordings) to set the bridge grace window and the cadence (every frame vs every 2nd).
4. Implement upper/lower-body HSV part histograms with confidence-weighted update; A/B against the current whole-box histogram on D-PTUAC look-alike sequences.
5. Characterise the two-model TPU path: weight reload time at ~35 MB/s, co-compilation feasibility within the 8 MB cache, and switch-wedge rate (W24-T9) — gate for tasks 6–7.
6. Prototype a FairMOT/JDE embedding head on the one-class YOLOv5n and measure int8 size, TPU time delta and re-ID R1 on D-PTUAC crops.
7. Validate box-height vs box-width vs ground-plane distance cues on recorded crouch / partial-occlusion clips; produce the error table the fusion memo needs.
8. Build the replay rig (virtual camera or s269 display path) so recorded sequences run through the real firmware for per-frame cost and lock metrics.
